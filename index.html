This document describes how the ontologies proposed by the CIRPASS-2 project for the Digital Product Passport (DPP) are organised: the EU DPP Core Ontology (CORE) with its modules, and the sectoral ontologies for batteries, tyres and textiles that build on it. It explains what each ontology covers and how they fit together, and links to the reference documentation of each one.

It defines no term. Classes and properties are documented in the reference pages, generated from the ontology files ().

This is an unofficial draft prepared within the CIRPASS-2 project [[CIRPASS-2]].

The ontologies it describes are proposals under development. Their terms, versions and metadata are documented only in the reference pages, which are regenerated from the ontology files whenever these change. This overview is written by hand and changes only when the architecture changes.

Introduction

CIRPASS-2 demonstrates Digital Product Passports in real settings, through pilots in several value chains [[CIRPASS-2]]. Within the project, a set of ontologies gives DPP data a shared, machine-readable meaning, within and across sectors. They are organised in two levels ():

  • the EU DPP Core Ontology (CORE) captures the cross-sectoral concepts identified in the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) [[ESPR]] and related acts, and is split into modules;
  • sectoral ontologies reuse the CORE and extend it with what a sector needs. This document covers the battery, tyre and textile ontologies.

How to read this documentation

The documentation has two parts.

  • This overview explains the architecture: why a CORE and modules, what each module covers, how the sectoral ontologies attach to the CORE, and which questions they are designed to answer.
  • The reference documentation has one page per ontology, generated with WIDOCO [[WIDOCO]] from the ontology file: every class and property with its definition, domain and range, an interactive WebVOWL graph, and the ontology in RDF/XML, Turtle, N-Triples and JSON-LD. The pages are listed in and in the index page.

For example, the meaning of the battery property batperf:ratedCapacity is given on the battery performance page; the reason why the battery ontology is split into ten modules is given in .

Namespaces

All CORE terms share one namespace, https://w3id.org/eudpp# (prefix eudpp), whatever their module. Each module has its own ontology IRI, https://w3id.org/eudpp/<MODULE>, for example https://w3id.org/eudpp/P_DPP. A single namespace keeps IRIs simple, at the cost of a higher risk of name collisions between modules: new names should be checked against the other modules.

These IRIs are permanent identifiers from w3id.org, redirected to the DPP Vocabulary Hub [[DPP-HUB]] where the ontologies are published. The proposed pattern for sectoral ontologies is https://w3id.org/eudpp/<name>, with a namespace of their own; lists the prefixes of the battery modules.

Architecture

The CORE is the semantic anchor of the sectoral ontologies: a sectoral ontology specialises CORE concepts, or refers to them, instead of redefining them. The ontologies give the meaning of the data. The content expected in a given passport (which data, with which cardinality and datatype) is described in application profiles, DPP templates and SHACL [[SHACL]] shapes, against which DPP data can be validated on the DPP Vocabulary Hub [[DPP-HUB]].

The architecture follows four principles.

  • Modularity. Each module covers one aspect of the regulation, following a modular ontology design [[MOMO]]. Modules are developed by different partners and versioned independently.
  • Alignment with the CORE. Sectoral ontologies extend, specialise or map CORE concepts rather than duplicating them. Concepts that several sectors need are proposed for the CORE.
  • Reuse before creation. Existing vocabularies are reused where possible, for example SI units in the P_DPP module and schema.org in the ACTOR module.
  • Traceability. Every term should carry a definition and a reference to its regulatory or domain source.

The EU DPP Core Ontology

The CORE file declares no term: it only imports the modules, using owl:imports [[OWL2-OVERVIEW]] (). Object properties whose domain and range belong to different modules are grouped in a dedicated module, CONNECTOR, so that the other modules do not depend on each other.

Modules imported by the CORE.
ModuleScope
P_DPPProducts, digital product passports and product properties, measurable or document-based. The central module, aligned with the draft European standard prEN 18223 on DPP system interoperability.
ACTORActors, their roles (economic operators, authorities, end users…) and facilities.
IDENTIdentifiers of products, passports, actors, facilities, materials and substances, with their schemes and granularity.
MATMaterials of composition and their origin. Material types are left to the sectors.
SOCSubstances and substances of concern, with concentrations and thresholds.
COMPThe EU declaration of conformity: applied Union harmonisation legislation, product requirements, conformity assessment modules and certificates.
EVENTEvents in the life cycle of a product and of its passport, inspired by EPCIS [[EPCIS]]. Draft.
CONNECTORObject properties linking the modules above.

A module for life cycle assessment (LCA) is under development and is not yet imported by the CORE. For reading convenience, the reference documentation also has a page merging all CORE files, CORE, complete vocabulary.

Sectoral ontologies

A sectoral ontology reuses the CORE and adds what the sector needs: sector products are declared as subclasses of eudpp:Product, sector characteristics as subclasses of eudpp:Property or of its subclasses, and concepts missing from the CORE are added locally. The method is described in the CIRPASS-2 guidelines for sector ontology engineering, inspired by LOT [[LOT]].

Three ways of attaching to the CORE are in use (): importing the CORE modules, importing MIREOT extracts (Minimum Information to Reference an External Ontology Term) that hold only the CORE terms actually reused, or referring to CORE terms by IRI without importing them. All three keep the sectoral terms aligned with the CORE. With a full import, the CORE axioms are loaded together with the sectoral ones; a MIREOT extract loads only the reused terms; with references by IRI, the sectoral files stay independent of the CORE files, and the battery ontology states in its metadata the CORE versions it was developed against.

How each sectoral ontology attaches to the CORE.
SectorAttachmentMain anchors in the CORE
BatteryRefers to CORE terms by IRI; no import. The battery class specialises eudpp:Product; performance and durability classes specialise eudpp:Property, eudpp:Durability and eudpp:ProductEvent; battery chemistry specialises eudpp:MaterialOfComposition. Category concepts are eudpp:ClassificationCode values; chemistry and metal concepts are eudpp:MaterialType values.
TyreImports the modules it reuses. The tyre class specialises eudpp:Product; tyre characteristics specialise eudpp:QuantitativeProperty or eudpp:ProductDimension.

Textile product classes specialise eudpp:Product; the textile substances of concern module specialises the SOC module; processes, repairs and recycling operations are linked to eudpp:ProductEvent. The extracts themselves (textile-dpp-mireot, textile-mat-mireot, textile-soc-mireot, textile-actor-mireot, textile-event-mireot, textile-si-mireot) are imported by the modules but are not part of the files delivered so far.

Battery

The battery ontology covers the battery passport of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 [[EU-BATTERIES]] (Article 77). Its attributes come from the regulation and from the BatteryPass-Ready data attribute long list, both cited as sources in the term annotations.

It is split into ten modules (): an umbrella module that declares the battery class, three thematic modules, and six controlled vocabularies in SKOS [[SKOS-REFERENCE]]. The vocabularies have their own modules because regulatory value lists change more often than the schema, so each can be versioned on its own.

The battery ontology declares no cardinality and no access rule. Which attributes are mandatory for each battery category, and which ones each audience may see (the public, persons with a legitimate interest, and notified bodies, market surveillance authorities and the Commission), are defined in a separate SHACL profile.

Tyre

The Example Tyre Ontology (ETO) was built to illustrate the CIRPASS-2 guidelines for sector ontology engineering. It is an example: it represents neither the tyre use case defined in CIRPASS-2 nor the future tyre ontology.

Its use case involves two roles, the manufacturer and the retreader. Tyre characteristics are grouped into dimensions, markings and labelling information.

Textile

The textile ontology, version 1.1.2, is a network of twelve modules and an umbrella file that imports them all (). It covers the textile product and its fibre composition, the processes it goes through, and the durability, repairability and recyclability information expected in a textile passport.

Its design keeps apart concerns that the earlier draft held together: characteristic types are separated from their values, test methods and results from the characteristics they measure, and derived indicators from the assessments they come from. A shared module, the textile connector, carries the patterns reused everywhere, quantitative values, reference periods and provenance vocabularies, so that the other modules do not depend on each other. Controlled value lists are SKOS concepts [[SKOS-REFERENCE]] declared in the module that uses them, under a codelist namespace of their own.

Use cases and competency questions

The functional requirements of the CORE are specified as competency questions in its requirements specification [[CO-REQUIREMENTS]]. gives, for the main use cases, examples of questions that each part of the architecture is designed to answer. The compliance questions are among those that guided the design of the COMP module; the others are illustrative. The competency questions have not yet been formally validated against product data.

Use cases and example competency questions.
Use caseExample questionsModules
Identification Which identifiers, and under which schemes, identify a product, its passport, its manufacturer and its facility? IDENT, P_DPP, ACTOR, CONNECTOR
Responsibility Which economic operator is responsible for the product, and in which role? ACTOR, P_DPP, CONNECTOR
Compliance Which Union harmonisation legislation is applied for the product? Which notified bodies assessed the product requirements, according to which conformity assessment modules? COMP, ACTOR, CONNECTOR
Composition Which materials is the product made of? Which substances of concern does it contain, and in which concentration? MAT, SOC, CONNECTOR
Traceability Which events has the product, or its passport, gone through, when and where? EVENT, CONNECTOR
Battery passport What are the category and the chemistry of a battery? What was its remaining capacity on a given date? Battery modules, P_DPP, MAT
Tyre retreading (example) Who retreaded a tyre, and what are its load index and speed symbol after retreading? ETO
Textile composition What is the fibre composition of a textile product? Textile core
Textile durability Which test method produced a durability result, and which indicator is derived from it? Textile assessment, indicator, durability
